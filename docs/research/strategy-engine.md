# Strategy engine

Chooses which ball to play, how to play it, and where the cue ball should end up, looking several shots ahead.

## The problem

- **State:** ball positions, which balls are legal targets, and the rules state. What is modelled is the game type and, in 8-ball, the open table or the player's group; ball in hand, fouls and the opponent's turn are not (see [Not built](#not-built-and-ideas)).
- **Action:** target ball, pocket, aim angle, speed, tip offset (side and vertical) and cue elevation.
- **Outcome:** uncertain, because nobody executes perfectly.

The planner maximises the chance of winning the rack (or, with a shorter horizon, of running the next *N* balls) for this player's skill.

## Execution-error model

What is built is described in detail under [Implemented in `:engine`](#implemented-in-engine); this part gives the shape of it.

Each player has one aim error: the standard deviation of the cue-line angle. The speed error is tied to it (about 6–10 % of the stroke speed), and side spin adds error through a misjudged allowance for squirt, swerve and throw. Tip-offset errors are not a separate input. Defaults come from the player profile ([calibration](calibration.md#player-profile)), and the player's calibration replaces them. Nothing is sampled at random:

- **P(make)** is computed in closed form: the normal probability mass of the cue-line angles that send the object ball into the pocket's entry window with both balls clear of every other ball, averaged over the speed error;
- **the cue ball's landing spread** comes from nine fixed perturbed strokes (aim, speed, tip), each simulated on the whole table: a cloud of nine resting places, the scratch probability and a spread radius.

## Candidate shots

For each legal target ball and each pocket:

1. Skip it if a path is blocked, the ghost ball is off the table or the cut is more than 78° (80° in the one-shot ranking).
2. Aim at the ghost ball with throw, squirt and swerve allowed for, then try a grid of strokes (on the first shot 7 speeds × 16 tips, deeper 3 speeds × 8 tips) and refine the best.
3. Banks, kicks, combinations and caroms are not part of the run-out search. When there is no direct pot, the fallback advisor (below) offers a two-ball combination, a one-cushion kick or a safety.

## Position value and heat zones

For a cue ball position *p*, the position value is the best make probability over the legal next balls and pockets, taken without a stroke (the window at a nominal arrival speed, no side spin). Drawn over a grid (16 × 8 cells by default, 32 × 16 for a plan's next ball) it is a **heat zone**: green where the next shot is easy, red where it is hard or blocked. The search evaluates the same value lazily, per 5 cm cell, only where its landings fall.

Looking ahead chains these values. As a design idea (a full dynamic programme over cue-ball positions, not built as such):

> value_k(p) = max over shots s of [ P(make s) × expected value_k+1(landing position of s) ]

What is built is a beam search that uses the landing cloud for the expectation and, at the end of the horizon, a one-shot look ahead in place of value_k+1 (the value of a plan, below).

## Search

- A depth-limited beam search over shots: 1–5 shots ahead, or in deep analysis as many shots as there are object balls, at most six, keeping the best 6 (deep analysis: 9) partial plans at each level. Each shot moves the table to its nominal result; the execution error enters through P(make), the scratch probability and how much of the next shot's chance the landing cloud keeps. Outcomes other than the planned one (a miss, a different leave) are not searched.
- No clock in the engine and no iterative deepening. The caller may pass a stop request, polled at every level, state, target–pocket pair and stroke; the search then returns the best complete plans it has found, marked as stopped. A time budget is the caller's to implement with it.
- Pruning that cannot change the answer: a target–pocket pair or a stroke whose make probability cannot lift its plan into the best of the level is skipped before it is simulated.
- Next-shot and look-ahead values are cached per table and per 5 cm cell: the value of landing at *p* does not depend on how the cue ball got there.
- **Misses** are not modelled beyond P(make): a missed shot simply ends the run-out. The opponent's chance from the layout left behind, and so safety play inside the search, are not built (the fallback advisor scores a safety by the pots it hides).

### Back-of-envelope cost

The design estimate was about 10 target–pocket pairs × 40 speed and spin combinations × 30 noisy samples, roughly 12,000 simulations per level, about 1 second per level on one core at 0.1 ms per simulation. The built search replaces the noisy samples with nine simulations per stroke and P(make) in closed form, and skips strokes that cannot matter; measured times are under [Implemented in `:engine`](#implemented-in-engine). Nothing has been measured on a phone yet.

## Game-specific rules

| Game | What changes |
|---|---|
| 8-ball | Choose a group while the table is open; choose the order; plan the key ball before the 8; the 8 must be last |
| 9-ball | Must hit the lowest-numbered ball first; any legal pocketed ball counts; combinations and caroms are common; push-out on the shot after the break |
| 10-ball | Same lowest-ball rule as 9-ball, but call shot: a ball that goes in by luck doesn't count |
| Snooker | Alternate a red and a colour; colours are re-spotted until the reds are gone, then potted in order; scoring by ball value; safety play is central |

Built: 8-ball (group choice on an open table, the order, the key ball before the 8, the 8 last) and the lowest-ball-first targets of 9-ball and 10-ball in `MultiShotPlanner`; snooker in its own planner, `SnookerStrategyPlanner` ([ADR-0004](../adr/0004-snooker-break-safety-planner-and-acoustic-break-speed.md)). Not built: the push-out after a 9-ball break, 10-ball call-shot handling (a shot that also drops another ball is simply not searched), and any foul or ball-in-hand logic.

## Problem balls

A ball is a problem when it has:

- **No pocket:** its position-value grid is close to zero everywhere the cue ball can reasonably reach;
- **Neighbours:** it touches, or is within about one ball width of, other balls that block its pocket paths;
- **A rail:** it is frozen to a cushion with only one realistic pocket.

### Breaking them up

- Look for shots that pocket a ball and send the cue ball into the cluster on its way to position (secondary break-outs), as well as direct break-out shots.
- A break-out counts only when the nominal simulation of the stroke moves a ball of the cluster; where the cue ball ends up comes from its landing cloud. How often it really opens the cluster is not estimated.
- **When:** break out early, while an easy *insurance ball* is still available to shoot next. Avoid break-outs that leave no shot. The planner compares "break out now" with "play on and break out later" through the same search.

## Explaining the advice

Every recommendation comes with a short reason, for example: "Play the 3 first: it clears the path to the 5, and the 6 is your insurance ball." The search keeps enough information to generate this.

## Validation

Done:

- **Self-play:** `MultiShotPlanner.evaluateAgainstGreedyBaseline` simulates racks and compares the beam search with a greedy "easiest shot next" baseline (`PlannerBenchmarkTest`).
- **Scenarios and invariants:** typed-in layouts with assertions a pool player would agree with (`PlannerScenarioTest`), and structural checks on random tables (`PlannerInvariantsTest`); both are described under [Implemented in `:engine`](#implemented-in-engine).

Not done (see [Not built](#not-built-and-ideas)): comparison with patterns from instruction books and experienced players, and player feedback on advice.

## Prior work

Computer pool players built for the Computer Olympiad pool events in the mid-2000s, notably PickPocket (Smith, 2007) and CueCard (Archibald et al., 2009), used similar ideas: sampling noisy shots, simulating them with physics, and searching a few shots ahead. See the [references](references.md).

## Implemented in `:engine`

See [ADR-0002](../adr/0002-event-spin-physics-and-beam-planner.md) for the design rationale behind the Milestone 1–3 strategy modules in `engine/src/commonMain/kotlin/com/runout/engine/planner/`:

- **One-shot & 2-ball baseline (`OneShotPlanner.kt`):** Ranks legal object-ball and pocket combinations across candidate speeds (or an explicit override) and vertical spin presets (`STUN`, `FOLLOW`, `DRAW`). A stroke's score is a product of factors that are each at most 1: its make probability (`makeProbability` at that speed and the preset's tip, reported as `ShotRecommendation.makeProbability`) × a centre term `0.90 + 0.10 c` × a position term `0.75 + 0.25 v` (`v` the stroke-free make probability of the next legal ball from where the cue ball stops, with small charges for cushions and draw; `0.58 + 0.42 v` when the next ball is the game ball) × `0.15` for a scratching path × `0.85` for a draw stroke when `maxEffectiveDrawRatio < 0.8` × `0.65` when the cue ball stops short. The score stays in `(0, 1]` without clipping. Before, the position bonus was added and the sum clipped at 1, so good pots tied at exactly 1.0 and were ordered by ball number (on 60 random open 8-ball tables, 57 had a top score of 1.0 and 49 a tie at the top; now none). On an open 8-ball table the next ball follows the group of the ball potted (`rulesAfterPotting`), and the 8 follows the last ball of that group.
- **Execution error and make probability (`ExecutionErrorModel.kt`):**
  - **The aim error is one number.** `PlayerProfile.aimErrorStdRad` is the standard deviation of the angle between the cue line the player meant and the one he played. The defaults are not measured: they are anchored to drill percentages estimated for each level (straight-in pots into a tournament corner pocket of a 9 ft table at pocket speed, `directionalMakeProbability` at the slow-ball jaw tolerance), one size per level fitted by least squares to two drills, and the player's own calibration replaces them:

    | Straight-in, cue ball to object ball / object ball to pocket | Beginner (`0.0059` rad, 0.34°) | Intermediate (`0.0025` rad, 0.14°) | Advanced (`0.00155` rad, 0.09°) |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | 1.0 m / 1.0 m (target about 1 in 3, 2 in 3, 9 in 10) | 0.32 | 0.67 | 0.88 |
    | 1.2 m / 1.2 m, the long straight-in drill (target 1 in 5, 1 in 2, 7 in 10) | 0.22 | 0.49 | 0.72 |
    | 0.5 m / 0.5 m (not fitted) | 0.92 | 1.00 | 1.00 |

    With the full model (speed error, arrival-speed tolerance) the same sizes give, for beginner / intermediate / advanced: a 30° cut over 1 m and 1 m 0.26 / 0.57 / 0.80; a 60° cut with 1.5 m to the ghost ball and 1 m to the pocket 0.09 / 0.20 / 0.31; a side-pocket straight-in over 0.5 m and 0.5 m 0.87 / 1.00 / 1.00; a side-pocket pot 30° off the rail over 0.4 m and 0.4 m 0.34 / 0.70 / 0.90 (`MakeProbabilityTest` prints the table).
  - **P(make) is a probability mass.** Each cue line meets the object ball at its own contact point, and the object ball leaves along the line of centres there (to first order its direction turns by the cue-line error times `d / (2R cos(cut))`, `d` from cue ball to ghost ball). The object ball drops when that direction lies inside the pocket's entry window (`PocketGeometry.entryWindow`) at the jaw tolerance of its arrival speed. Each edge of the window is mapped back exactly, through the ghost-ball geometry, to the cue-line angle that sends the ball along it (an edge beyond a grazing hit maps to the graze), and P(make) is the normal probability mass between those two angles (`directionalMakeProbability`). The window is in general not centred on the aimed line; the integral takes each side as it is. **Other balls narrow it.** A ball beside the object ball's path removes the directions along which the object ball would touch it before reaching the pocket's capture line (a cone of half-angle `asin(2R / L)` around the direction to it, cut back where the contact would come after the capture line); a ball beside the cue ball's path removes, in the same way, the cue lines on which the cue ball touches it before the object ball. What is left can be several intervals, and P(make) is the mass of all of them. A pot over 1.17 m with a neighbour 1 mm outside the object ball's corridor drops from 0.86 to 0.46 (0.59 at 5 mm, 0.82 at 15 mm, unchanged at 30 mm), matching a direct numerical integration of the cue-line error; before, only the centre line was checked. The cue ball after contact, balls set moving during the shot and cushions are not part of this. Speed error decides whether the object ball reaches the pocket's capture line and how fast it arrives (and so the jaw tolerance); it is integrated with a three-point Gauss-Hermite rule over the speed fraction (`makeProbability`). The speed error is tied to the aim error (`0.045 + 9.2 x aimErrorStdRad`): about 10 %, 7 % and 6 % of the stroke speed for the beginner, intermediate and advanced profiles, also estimates.
  - **Side spin costs accuracy.** A player using side has to allow for squirt, swerve and the spin part of the throw, and misjudges that allowance by 25 % of its size (one standard deviation, a modelling value), added to the aim error in quadrature (`cueLineErrorStdRad`).
  - **One function, two forms.** Every make probability is `directionalMakeProbability`. A shot with a stroke (each step of a plan, each stroke of the one-shot ranking) uses `makeProbability`, with its speed (reach and arrival-speed tolerance) and tip (side-spin error). Where no stroke has been chosen yet (position maps, the search's next-shot, hold and look-ahead values, the one-shot ranking's next-ball term) the stroke-free form `HeatZoneGenerator.shotQuality` takes the window at the nominal arrival speed with the player's plain aim error: a deliberate, cheaper proxy.
  - **Landing cloud.** `evaluateShotUncertainty` still runs the deterministic 9-point sigma-point stencil over (cue-line angle, speed fraction, side tip, vertical tip); every point is simulated with the full table simulation from its own exact contact geometry (a different side tip also changes the squirt). It gives `scratchProbability`, the 9-point `landingCloud` (and which of its points scratch), `meanLandingPosition` and a 95 % `landingSpreadRadiusMeters`. It no longer feeds P(make).
  - `pocketAcceptanceHalfAngleRadians` is the half-angle of that entry window at a given arrival speed, a readout for tests and display (the make probability integrates the window itself). It narrows with distance, with a shallower approach to the mouth and with speed, and closes altogether when the ball cannot pass between the points (a side pocket from 1 m closes at about 22° to the rail for a slow ball and 28° for a fast one; a corner pocket stays open down to a ball running along the rail).
- **Position maps (`HeatZoneMap.kt`):** `HeatZoneGenerator.computeForNextBalls` builds a grid (`16 × 8` by default) scoring each playable cue-ball cell with the best make probability on the next target ball (a `0.75 / 0.25` blend when a second target is given), with $2.05R$ ball-overlap exclusion and line-of-sight shadows. It draws the heat-zone overlay in the UI; the search evaluates its leaves lazily instead (below).
- **Problem-ball & breakout detection (`ProblemBallAnalyzer.kt`):** Scans legal targets (and the 8-ball in 8-ball) to flag `NO_CLEAR_POCKET` (zero unobstructed pockets), `CLUSTERED_NEIGHBOURS` (neighbours within $2.8 R$), and `FROZEN_TO_RAIL` (within $R + 3\,\text{mm}$ of a cushion with $\le 1$ open pocket). Picks the cleanest open **insurance ball** ($\ge 2$ clear pockets, zero neighbours within $2.8 R$) and detects whether a cue-ball path passes within $2.3 R$ of a problem ball (`checkTrajectoryBreakout`, measured along the sampled curve of the path rather than its chords).
- **Multi-shot beam planner (`MultiShotPlanner.kt`):**
  - Searches `1..5` shots ahead (`beamWidth = 6`) or, with `deepAnalysis`, as many shots as there are object balls but at most six (`beamWidth = 9`); deep analysis covers the whole rack only when six or fewer balls are left. An 8-ball player moves to the `8` once the group is cleared.
  - **The reported probability** (`cumulativeRunoutProbability`, shown as "Run-Out" and "Next N shots") is P = m₁(1 − s₁) × h₁ × m₂(1 − s₂) × h₂ × … × mₙ(1 − sₙ): each shot's make and no-scratch probability, times, for every shot but the last, its *hold* hᵢ (below, stored as `positionHoldProbability`). It reads P(make 1) × P(position held | made) × P(make 2 | nominal position) × …; the hold is a share of the next shot's chance, not a second make probability, so nothing is counted twice. It never exceeds the product of makes. Before, the hold was only in the ranking value, so the number shown was the product of makes (on the scenario layouts S3, S8 and S9 the reported figure is now 0.89, 0.88 and 0.85 where the product of makes is 0.93, 0.95 and 1.00).
  - **The value of a plan**, used for ranking, is that probability times the last shot's hold, times the product of control over its shots, times the leave (the same product as before, so the ranking did not change):
    - *Control* is one factor per stroke, each cause counted once: a cue-ball route of two cushions (×0.90) or three (×0.80, then −0.05 per cushion, floor 0.60); a tip offset beyond `0.25 R` toward the miscue limit (down to ×0.80 at the limit); a speed above 6.5 (−5 % per unit) or below 2.0 (−4 % per unit). The game-winning shot needs no position, so its cushions cost nothing. Cut, distance and side spin are not in it: they are in P(make).
    - *Hold* is the share of the next shot's make probability that survives the player's error: the next shot's best make probability averaged over the non-scratching points of the landing cloud, divided by its value at the nominal landing (at most 1).
    - *Leave*, inside the horizon, is the next shot's best make probability from the landing. At the end of the horizon it is a **one-shot look ahead** (`leafValue`): for the two best next pots from the landing, the make probability times the best leave it gives on the ball after, over six strokes (centre, follow and draw at speeds 3 and 5, each traced among the other balls for up to two cushions and charged for cushions like *control*). Rotation games look at the next ball in order, 8-ball at the best remaining ball. When the next ball is the last one, the leave is its make probability. Next-shot and leaf values are cached lazily per table (object balls to 5 mm) and per 5 cm cell.
    - *8-ball rack term:* each ball of the player's group (or the 8) still flagged by the problem-ball analysis on the table a shot leaves multiplies the leave by `0.85`, so a break-out played now, while other balls of the group remain to be played after it, beats one put off past the horizon. The explanations name the ball that is still tied up.
  - **Strokes.** A coarse grid first (7 speeds × 16 tips on the first shot, 3 speeds × 8 tips deeper; the deeper tips are the first shot's centre-line and mild follow-side tips, so a planned step can be reproduced when it becomes the first shot), then **refinement** of the best stroke of each target-pocket pair (and of the distinct-route alternatives kept before the last ply): the speed moves in 0.5 steps in each direction while the plan improves (up to six steps, within 1 to 9.5), then one 0.09 step of the vertical tip either way. A pinned speed or tip is not refined. When two strokes give plans within 0.2 % of each other, the one at a pace closer to 4 (a medium speed) and then closer to centre ball is played. Before the last ply, two further cue-ball routes per target-pocket pair (one on later plies) are kept besides the best, each landing at least 25 cm from the others: when the next pot is easy from all of them, only the plies after can tell them apart.
  - **Rejected shots.** A shot is only searched if its nominal simulation pots the target in the intended pocket, drops no other ball (no plan counts on a carom, and in 8-ball an early 8 loses), and the nominal cue ball does not scratch, and if its scratch probability stays below 0.45; the simulations of a stroke stop as soon as one of these fails. Each step records the first three as `legalAsPlanned`, and `ShotUncertaintyResult.targetBallPotted` means potted in the intended pocket. When the caller allows a first shot that fails these (`allowHighScratchFirstShot`, used to show the result of a user's edit), it ends the plan with the leave floor and always ranks below every plan that pots.
  - **Pruning that cannot change the result.** A child's value is at most its parent's value without the leave times its shot's make probability. Target–pocket pairs are expanded in order of an upper bound on their make probability (the centred normal mass of the widest window, at the lenient jaw tolerance and the plain aim error); once the level has `beamWidth` legal children, a pair whose bound cannot reach the weakest of them is skipped, and so is a stroke whose own make probability (computed before its nine simulations) cannot. Such a child could never have survived the level. Checked on 24 random tables of the three games: the five returned plans are identical with and without it.
  - **Stopping.** `planRunout` takes an optional `shouldStop: () -> Boolean`, polled at every level, state, target–pocket pair and stroke (a stroke costs up to nine simulations). When it returns true the search returns at once with the best complete plans found so far, including those of the level it was in, marked `searchStopped` (and without the heat zone); `planRunoutWithStatus` also says whether it stopped. A hook that never fires changes nothing. The engine keeps no clock: a deadline, or a newer table replacing the one being planned, is the caller's to express in the hook.
  - Produces ranked `RunoutPlan` sequences with the reported run-out probability, `nextBallHeatZone`, `problemBalls`, and plain-English `explanationReasons`, including what the one-shot look ahead saw at the end of the plan. `positionSuccessProbability` on a step is the probability of not scratching and then making the next shot from where the cue ball stops, averaged over the landing cloud. An optional `firstStepNumber` numbers the steps from a later shot.
  - **Measured** on the JVM (`PlannerBenchmarkTest`, median of five runs after warm-up; the machine was shared with other builds, so runs vary by up to a factor of two): a 3-shot plan takes 75-140 ms on a full nine-ball rack, 95-176 ms on a nine-ball photo layout, 130-300 ms on an 8-ball table with five solids left, and 420-540 ms on an open 15-ball 8-ball table (about 930 ms before the pruning and the early stop of illegal strokes; 460-730 ms with solids chosen, against about 1,170 ms). Deep analysis (six shots) takes 160-280 ms on the nine-ball rack and 0.85-1.25 s on the 15-ball table (about 2.6 s before). A stop request is honoured within a millisecond, because the hook is polled before every stroke is simulated. Each stroke that is simulated costs up to nine full table simulations (its landing cloud); the leaf adds up to twelve single-ball traces for each cache cell it has not seen. Nothing has been measured on a phone.
  - Includes `evaluateAgainstGreedyBaseline` to verify across multi-ball racks that beam-search planning with 2D spin and speed control achieves a strictly higher run-out rate than a fixed-speed, zero-english greedy 1-shot baseline.
  - **Scenarios** (`PlannerScenarioTest`) are typed-in layouts with assertions a pool player would agree with: a stop shot when the next ball is close; the right side of the ball; no draw with side when a natural stroke gets there; a shallow side-pocket pot rated Hard; a down-the-rail pot rated lower at speed; a hanger; an 8-ball break-out while the insurance ball is still on the table; the key ball for the 8; bringing the cue ball up-table when the easy balls are at the foot; skill ordering; and three nine-ball layouts typed from photos of real tables. Each checks a precondition that makes its assertion meaningful (for example that draw with side would also get position, or that the shallow side pot exists). `PlannerInvariantsTest` checks every plan on sixty random tables of the three games: legal targets in order, each step legal as planned, probabilities in range, and the reported probability equal to its definition.

### No direct pot (`FallbackShotAdvisor.kt`)

When the legal ball cannot be potted directly, or only with a make probability below `FallbackShotAdvisor.DIRECT_POT_FLOOR` (0.20, a modelling choice), `FallbackShotAdvisor.advise` gives one recommendation of one of three kinds. This is a first step: one recommendation chosen by a fixed rule, no search over several shots, and no model of the opponent beyond "has a direct pot or not" (the fuller version would estimate the opponent's chance of running out from the leave). No banks, caroms, jumps or massé.

- **Two-ball combination.** The legal ball goes at the ghost position of a second ball for a pocket (`PocketGeometry.aimPoint`), with all three legs clear, the first contact cut at most 75° and the second at most 60°. Rotation games allow any second ball; 8-ball only the shooter's own group (never an opponent's ball, never the 8 early). Throw at both contacts is allowed for in the aim (`BallBallContact.lineOfCentresForThrownDirection`), and the ball speeds come from the contact law and the cloth. The make probability propagates the cue-line error exactly through both contacts: to first order the second ball's direction turns by the error times `d1 / (2R cos(cut1))` times `d2 / (2R cos(cut2))`, so a combination is much less forgiving than either pot alone. The change of throw with the error is neglected. The probability is the normal mass of cue-line angles that keep the second ball inside the pocket's entry window at its arrival-speed jaw tolerance, averaged over the player's speed error; three speeds are tried.
- **Kick to hit.** Used when no line of contact on the legal ball is clear, or when a direct hit exists but no legal safety is found. For each cushion, centre-ball directions within 12° of the mirror-image line are traced every 0.25° with the table simulation (the real cushion rebound and cloth), at two speeds. A direction counts when the cue ball meets the legal ball after exactly one cushion, touches no other ball on the way, does not drop in a pocket, and reaches a cushion again after contact. The aim is the middle of the longest run of good directions; the chance of a legal hit is the normal mass of the cue-line error over the run around the aim, at the three speeds of the speed error.
- **Basic safety.** When the legal ball can be hit but not potted: seven lines of contact (full, ±20°, ±40°, ±60°), three speeds and centre ball or draw are simulated on the whole table. A stroke qualifies when the cue ball hits the legal ball first, nothing drops and a ball reaches a cushion after the contact. The leave is scored by the share of the opponent's (target ball, pocket) pairs hidden from where the cue ball stops (`HeatZoneGenerator.computePocketVisibility` and `isPointOccludedForPocket` on the resulting table). For the three best, nine perturbed strokes (speed and line of contact) give the chance that every pair is hidden.
- **Selection.** A combination at or above a 40 % make chance (a modelling choice) beats a safety; a safety beats a kick; a kick is offered when there is no direct hit or no legal safety is found. A weaker combination is offered only when neither a safety nor a kick is found. When the run-out planner has a pot at or above the floor, there is no advice; when the best pot is weaker than that (the run-out planner may then still return a plan), the advice is offered beside it and its reason names that pot's make probability.
- The app shows the advice as one card in place of the recommended shot and draws its lines on the table; the rolling plan itself plans only pots, so it ends where no pot exists.

### Rolling plan (`RollingRunoutPlanner.kt`)

The search above plans a fixed horizon from one table. `RollingRunoutPlanner` lets the user walk that plan shot by shot without losing the look-ahead. It is a pure `state -> state` layer over `MultiShotPlanner.planRunoutWithStatus`; it adds no new search.

- **Window.** `RollingPlanState` holds the frozen `acceptedSteps` and a `window` of the next `horizon` shots from the focus (`focus = acceptedSteps.size`). On a 9-ball rack with a 3-shot horizon the window is balls 1-3; accepting shot 1 plans a full horizon from the table that shot leaves, so the window becomes 2-4, then 3-5. Near the end of the rack the window is shorter; after the game ball it is `null` and `isRackComplete` is true. `PlannedShotStep.stepNumber` counts from the base table (accepted plus window), and "Step n" mentions in the explanations are shifted to match.
- **Accepted steps are frozen.** They are never re-planned; the table and rules at the focus come from the last accepted step's `resultingLayout` and `rulesAfterPotting` folded over the accepted targets, so accepting a solid on an open 8-ball table assigns solids for the rest of the window.
- **Overrides.** `setOverride` pins any of tip, speed, pocket and target ball on the focused step (the planner's `customFirstShot*` arguments) and re-plans, so later steps re-fit around it. An edit that pots nothing still produces a window so the user can see the result, but `acceptAndAdvance` refuses it with a reason (`advanceRefusal`): the cue ball stops short, the ball is not potted, it drops in another pocket than the plan's, the cue ball scratches, another ball drops with it in 8-ball (the 8, an opponent's ball, or any step flagged as not `legalAsPlanned`), or there is no resulting table to continue from. In rotation games (9-ball, and 10-ball since call-shot is not modelled), an edited shot that pots the target in its planned pocket without scratching while also dropping another ball is accepted: the plan continues from the simulated table with every pocketed ball removed, or finishes the rack if the game ball dropped.
- **Stepping back.** `back` reopens the previous step with exactly the view that was shown before it was accepted and loads its choices as the override; accepting again without an edit restores the later view unchanged.
- **Stability.** Steps the user has already seen are not reshuffled by every advance. After accepting, the fresh best plan is compared with the best plan whose first shot is the previously shown next shot (same target, pocket, tip and speed, obtained by passing it as the custom first shot). The fresh plan replaces it only when its `expectedValueScore` is at least 5 % higher. Whatever then differs from what was shown is reported in two sets: `updatedStepNumbers` for a different target ball or pocket (a route change) and `refinedStepNumbers` for the same target and pocket with a different tip or speed (a stroke refinement). Both are stored with the view, so stepping back and forward restores them, and the same split is reported when an edit re-fits the later steps.
- **User edits.** `userEditedStepNumbers` (numbers counted from the base) records the steps the user explicitly overrode, so the UI can mark them; accepted steps keep their mark. It is separate from `focusOverride`, which `back` also fills with the reopened step's own choices, and `isFocusEdited` tells the two apart. Clearing an override after `back` drops the pinned choices and returns to the planner's own choice for that step. Stepping back leaves the marks unchanged; editing an earlier step discards the restored future and prunes the marks of steps that no longer exist; `start` and `rebase` clear them.
- **Moving a ball.** `rebase` clears the accepted steps and makes the edited table the new base.
- **Cost.** One planner call per action, two when the fresh plan and the shown continuation disagree. Results are memoised in an immutable cache carried in the state (`PlanCache`, up to 16 entries), keyed by table, rules, player profile, horizon, deep-analysis mode, override and non-potting allowance.

## Not built, and ideas

Design thoughts that the code does not implement. They are kept as research, not as a description of the app.

- **The opponent and safety play inside the search.** The search values a leave by the player's own next shots only; the opponent's chance from the layout left behind is not estimated. The fallback advisor scores a safety by the pots it hides, with no opponent model beyond "has a direct pot or not". A fuller version would estimate the opponent's chance of running out from the leave and weigh it against the player's own.
- **Banks, kicks, combinations and carom in the run-out search.** Only direct pots are searched; combinations and kicks appear only in the fallback advice, and banks, caroms, jumps and massé are not offered at all.
- **Misses and what follows.** A missed shot ends the run-out; the layouts a miss leaves are not searched or valued.
- **Fouls, ball in hand and the 9-ball push-out.** The rules state holds the game type and the 8-ball group only.
- **A full position value function.** The recursion for `value_k(p)` above, solved over a grid of cue-ball positions, is the idea; the built search uses a beam over strokes with a one-shot look-ahead at its end.
- **Iterative deepening and a time budget in the engine.** The search has a stop hook and no clock; a deadline is the caller's to write.
- **Timings on a phone.** The measured times are from the JVM on a development machine.
- **Player-facing validation.** Comparing the advice with patterns from instruction books and experienced players, and letting players mark advice as good or bad, are not done.
